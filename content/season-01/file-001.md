## Core premise (one sentence)
At 03:17 UTC every phone on Earth says `DON'T LOOK AT THE MOON.`, and the people who look get an answer.

## Brief
- Runtime: 28–35 s (script runs ≈ 33 s).
- Simple international English. Narration ≈ 50 words.
- Hook within 1.5 s: phones light up at the same instant in three different local times, with the opening line starting at frame 1.
- Causal link: **looking at the Moon → receiving `WE SAW YOU TOO.`** It is shown visually (someone who did not look gets nothing) and stated once in narration.
- Ending: `FILE #001 — ARCHIVED` (editorial overlay, not an in-world message).
- No Archivist. Neutral narrator.

## Narrative rules (carried from canon decisions)
- 03:17 is UTC, one global instant. Local times and daylight differ (D1).
- Only people who looked receive `WE SAW YOU TOO.`, which stays unexplained: no reveal of who "we" are (D2).
- The in-world sender never references `FILE #` numbering. The sting is an editorial overlay (D3).
- No Archivist narration, voice or appearance (D4).
- Phone screens use only the fictional anomaly-message interface; never imitate real emergency alerts or government systems (D6).
- Do not imply real news. Keep geography generic. No real agency branding.
- The Moon stays visually recognizable and has the same phase in every shot.

## Sound direction
- scene-01: tight cascade of simultaneous vibrations and notification sounds, all original. No real OS notification sounds.
- scene-02: low atmospheric drone begins.
- scene-05: hard digital dropout, then near silence.
- scene-06: single deep impact on the Moon reveal.
- scene-07: **second-message tone**: distinct, unnerving and clearly different from the first notification (see the interface spec). Must not resemble emergency-alert attention signals or any OS/carrier tone.
- scene-08: silence holding on the message.
- scene-09: short low archive "stamp" sound.
